# Sustainability Committee — annual review of the transition plan

Minute extract, approved by the Committee and noted by the Board.

## Purpose of the review

The Committee reviews the transition plan annually against three questions: whether the
targets remain consistent with the pathway they were set against, whether the levers are
delivering at the rate assumed, and whether any assumption recorded in the annex has failed.
The review is minuted whether or not it results in a change, because a plan that is only
documented when it changes cannot be shown to have been reviewed.

## Governance of the plan within the undertaking

Accountability sits with the Chief Operating Officer. The Committee reports to the Board twice
a year and has standing authority to require a revision. No capital expenditure outside the
approved plan may be committed without Board consent, which is the mechanism that keeps the
plan connected to what the undertaking actually spends rather than to what it intends.

Executive remuneration is linked to the emissions trajectory. The Committee reviews that
linkage each year and records whether the measure used remains the one the plan is stated
against, since a target and an incentive that drift apart are worse than an incentive that was
never set.

## Findings of this review

The pathway alignment is unchanged. Fleet replacement is proceeding at the assumed rate in the
urban distribution fleet and behind it on trunk routes, for the reason recorded in the annex:
charging availability outside the undertaking's own depots. The Committee has asked for that
dependency to be reported to the Board separately rather than netted into the overall
trajectory.

Depot electrification is ahead of the sequence agreed with the network operator. The power
purchase agreement is signed and its coverage is as assumed.

## What the Committee did not conclude

The Committee did not conclude that the target will be met. It concluded that the plan remains
the undertaking's best route to it and that the dependencies which could prevent it are
identified, monitored and disclosed. That distinction is deliberate and is repeated in the
statement.
