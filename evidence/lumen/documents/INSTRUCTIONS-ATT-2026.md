# Instructions for use — operator guidance

Issued with the system to every deployer, as the AI Act requires. This document tells the
people who operate the system what it will and will not do, and what they are expected to do
about it.

## What the operator is responsible for

The operator accepts or returns every drafted narrative. Acceptance is not a formality and is
recorded against a named person: the system will not issue a report without it, and there is
no configuration that removes the step. An operator who accepts a draft is asserting that they
compared it against the passages cited beneath it.

## How the system presents its work for review

Each drafted paragraph is shown beside the evidence passages it rests on, with the identifier
of each passage against the sentence that used it. Figures are not shown as prose; they appear
as resolved values carrying the identifier of the datapoint they came from, so that checking a
number means following it to its source rather than trusting the sentence around it.

Where the system has refused, the refusal is shown in the place the disclosure would have
occupied, with its reason. An operator therefore sees what is missing in the same document as
what is present, rather than having to consult a separate log to discover that something was
withheld.

## When to stop and escalate

Stop and escalate where a refusal recurs across periods, where a drafted narrative asserts
something the operator cannot find in the cited passages, and where evidence appears that
addresses the reading system rather than describing the undertaking. The last of these is
recorded by the system as a finding on the document; it is not a malfunction and it should not
be treated as one.

## What the operator must not do

An operator may not grant an override; that authority belongs to the approver named in the
human oversight procedure. An operator may not edit a resolved figure. An operator may not
issue a report in which a datapoint is unresolved, and the system will not permit it — the
instruction is stated here because a control the operator understands is a control the
operator does not try to work around.
